# Saint-Paul-de-Vence: the art of living in an artists’ village

**Excerpt:** Between historic ramparts, studios, galleries and the Fondation Maeght, Saint-Paul-de-Vence combines heritage, creativity and Riviera countryside living.

A short distance inland from the Mediterranean, Saint-Paul-de-Vence offers a different way of experiencing the French Riviera. This fortified hilltop village brings together heritage, artistic creation and Mediterranean landscapes. Beyond its postcard appeal, that distinctive identity attracts people looking for a home in the Riviera hinterland.

The village’s sixteenth-century ramparts still define its silhouette. Beyond the gates, cobbled lanes, intimate squares and stone façades invite exploration on foot. The historic centre offers a preserved architectural setting and a pace of life quite different from the larger coastal resorts.

Art here is not simply a memory. Along Rue Grande and the surrounding streets, galleries and workshops run by painters, sculptors and ceramicists sustain a visible creative scene. Marc Chagall lived in Saint-Paul-de-Vence from 1966 to 1985, an important chapter in an artistic story that continues through contemporary work.

Close to the old village, the Fondation Maeght is a landmark for modern and contemporary art. Its collections, exhibitions and architecture complement the galleries and artists’ studios and contribute to the village’s cultural identity.

Living here also means enjoying hills, olive trees and countryside paths that evoke Provence while remaining connected to the Riviera. As in any historic village, travel, parking and access to local services need to be assessed carefully for each address and lifestyle.

For a home purchase, a property’s charm is only part of the picture: orientation, immediate surroundings, village access and everyday practicalities matter as much as architectural character.
